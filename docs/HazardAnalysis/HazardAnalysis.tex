\section{Introduction}

This document covers the hazard analysis for PronouncedFish, a web-based
assistant for speech-language pathologists (SLPs) during supervised guided
practice and testing. The system captures and analyzes a child's speech
during the session and flags substitutions, omissions, and additions. It
presents results and visualizations through the SLP dashboard. Testing uses
Goldman--Fristoe items, and guided practice uses separate items that exclude
the test words. The system assists the SLP in exercising professional judgment.
This document defines hazard as a property or condition in the system together with a condition in the environment that has the 
potential to cause harm or loss. 
The purpose of this document is to identify the hazards of the system, so mitigation strategies and safety requirements can be 
derived. 

\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis}

This hazard analysis addresses the major hazards that could negatively impact PronouncedFish. This includes hazards with 
speech/audio capture, speech analysis, and interfaces. The analysis will cover the various risks such as with patient data handling, 
data privacy, accuracy, and performance. The losses that could result from the hazards include violations of the Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA), privacy breaches, inaccurate analysis of speech and presentation of data, and loss of stakeholder trust.
The purpose of this hazard analysis is early identification of any risks that could affect the key components of the system. 
Identifying these risks will inform the creation of mitigation strategies and proper safety and security requirements. 
Doing this will minimize risk and potential loss from unaddressed hazards.

\section{Critical Assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The child attempts the requested speech prompt during the supervised session.
    \item A trained SLP supervises the child and operates the clinician functions.
    \item The SLP uses Safari on an iPad with a microphone and access to the local server.
    \item The system is intended for use in Ontario.
    \item The SLP is familiar with web-based software.
    \item The SLP uses professional judgment to interpret the system's findings.
\end{itemize}

\section{Safety and Security Requirements}

The following security, safety, and reliability requirements address the
failure modes identified in Section~\ref{sec:fmea}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA1}{\textbf{FR-HA1}}: When a recording fails the usability checks for noise, clipping, or duration
(SRS FR-AC3), the system shall flag it to the SLP and offer a retry. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can address the recording issue and request
another attempt. Addresses H1.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA2}{\textbf{FR-HA2}}: For clinical release, the system shall back up patient progress data and
analytics at the end of each session and alert the SLP if the backup fails. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Backups support recovery from storage failure or
corruption. Failure alerts identify when a backup is unavailable. Addresses H7.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA3}{\textbf{FR-HA3}}: The system shall report phoneme confidence and an attempt-level confidence
score to the SLP. It shall mark attempts below the threshold established in
the validation study as unclassifiable (SRS FR-SA3). \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can identify results that need additional review,
and the system avoids forcing a classification below the threshold.
Addresses H1 and H8.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA4}{\textbf{FR-HA4}}: When a connection check or recording request shows
that the local server is unreachable, the system shall notify the SLP and
prevent new recording attempts until the connection is restored.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can tell that recording is unavailable, and
the system avoids storing offline recordings on the iPad. Addresses H3.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA5}{\textbf{FR-HA5}}: If the connection is lost before an attempt is
confirmed saved, the system shall mark the attempt interrupted. When the
connection returns, the system shall check whether the attempt was saved on
the server. Completed attempts already saved shall be preserved. If the
interrupted attempt was unsaved, the system shall allow the SLP to retry it.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Checking the server before a retry prevents the same
attempt from being recorded twice and protects already saved results. Addresses H3 and H7.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA6}{\textbf{FR-HA6}}: The system shall require authorized SLPs to sign in with two-factor authentication before they can access the clinician dashboard, patient records, or stored recordings. The system shall provide password recovery for SLP accounts.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Two-factor authentication reduces the risk of unauthorized patient data access if a password is compromised. Password recovery lets authorized SLPs regain access to their accounts. Addresses H4, H6.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA7}{\textbf{FR-HA7}}: The clinician dashboard shall identify automated results and visualizations
as assistive and subject to the SLP's professional interpretation (SRS FR-RDV7). \\
\textbf{Rationale:} An automated result may be incorrect and should be
reviewed by the SLP before it informs care. Addresses H8.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA8}{\textbf{FR-HA8}}: The system shall prevent recording until the
authenticated SLP confirms that valid consent covers recording, retention,
and later playback for the selected patient and session.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} The clinic documents consent through its existing process.
The SLP confirms that it applies before the application captures audio. Addresses H4.

\item \hypertarget{ha:FR-HA9}{\textbf{FR-HA9}}: The system shall audit each view, creation, modification, export, and
deletion of a patient record. Each event shall identify the action, type of
information, date and time, user, and patient reference. The system shall
retain the deletion event and earlier events after the patient record is
deleted, and make the audit log available to authorized users (SRS FR-DMR4
and FR-DMR5). \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Authorized users can investigate patient-record access
and changes after a record is deleted. Addresses H4 and H7.

\end{itemize}

\section{Roadmap}

The Expo release includes recording-quality feedback (FR-HA1), confidence
reporting (FR-HA3), connection-loss handling and retry (FR-HA4 and FR-HA5),
SLP authentication and password recovery (FR-HA6), assistive interpretation
guidance (FR-HA7), and SLP confirmation of recording consent (FR-HA8).
Expo also includes patient-record auditing (FR-HA9), deletion confirmation,
and retention of the audit trail after deletion (SRS NFR-USA2 and FR-DMR4).
These controls support supervised sessions and restrict access to patient
data.

Session-end backup and failure alerts (FR-HA2) are addressed for clinical
release.

\section{Appendix --- Reflection}

\input{../Reflection.text}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What went well while writing this deliverable?
    We went into this deliverable with a general idea of the expected hazards.
    In many of our prior discussions (both as a group and with our supervisors), we discussed many of the potential failure modes.
    As a result, we spent more time expanding on the failure modes we have previously identified rather than ideating from scratch.
    Our supervisors also gave us many jumping off points to identify failures and provided us with related reading material.
    \item What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how
    did you resolve them?
    We struggled with differentiating on what is a failure mode and what is actually a cause of the failure mode.
    For instance, we identified the potential issue of unique vocal patterns or accents causing an incorrect phoneme to be identified.
    However, we realized that the unmet requirement was that an incorrect phoneme was identified (accuracy).
    This meant that it was ultimately a redundancy in the failure mode: Phonemic identification model misclassifies patient speech.
    To resolve this, we started rooting out the specific requirements that were not met in our ideas.
    We then separated the cause of failure out of this statement to reorganize our thoughts.
    \item Which of your listed risks had your team thought of before this
    deliverable, and which did you think of while doing this deliverable? For
    the latter ones (ones you thought of while doing the Hazard Analysis), how
    did they come about?
    We have had many discussions about risks associated with phonemic identification both in terms of accuracy and performance.
    We also had general discussions about sensitive patient data being lost or accessed by an actor who should not be able to access it.
    However, during the actual identification of the failure modes, we separated the data loss and incorrect access into more subtypes, such as a data breach or authentication failure.
    We then identified two additional risks, which were replacement of a clinician's judgement and patient frustration.
    We came up with these during analysis of our non-functional requirements, specifically medical requirements for the clinician's judgement and usability requirements for patient frustration.
    \item Other than the risk of physical harm (some projects may not have any
    appreciable risks of this form), list at least 2 other types of risk in
    software products. Why are they important to consider?
    One type of risk is the spread of misinformation. Many software products are used to provide people with media access.
    If the information shared by that media is incorrect or exaggerated, it can lead to people making drastic decisions that are not in their best interest or are unsafe.
    Another type of risk is a data breach. Software products are frequently entrusted with people's data.
    If that data is a vulnerability for them, it can be used to take advantage of the person, or exposes something that puts them into danger.
    For instance, if an app has information about banking information, the exposure of that information can lead to someone accessing their finances.

\end{enumerate}
