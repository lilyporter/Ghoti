\section{Introduction}

This document covers the hazard analysis for the Ghoti project, a web-based software assistant for speech-language pathologist (SLPs) 
during sessions. The system captures and analyses a child’s speech in real-time, and flags it for substitution errors. Results are 
presented to the SLP through a dashboard interface and data visualizations. The system also offers in-session exercises to support 
the SLP in coaching a patient on speech challenges. It is strictly assistive and does not replace the professional judgement of the 
SLP. 
This document defines hazard as a property or condition in the system together with a condition in the environment that has the 
potential to cause harm or loss. 
The purpose of this document is to identify the hazards of the system, so mitigation strategies and safety requirements can be 
derived. 

\section{Scope and Purpose of Hazard Analysis}

This hazard analysis addresses the major hazards that could negatively impact the Ghoti project. This includes hazards with 
speech/audio capture, speech analysis, and interfaces. The analysis will cover the various risks such as with patient data handling, 
data privacy, accuracy, and performance. The losses that could result from the hazards include violations of PHIPA, privacy breaches, inaccurate analysis of speech and presentation of data, and loss of stakeholder trust.
The purpose of this hazard analysis is early identification of any risks that could affect the key components of the system. 
Identifying these risks will inform the creation of mitigation strategies and proper safety and security requirements. 
Doing this will minimize risk and potential loss from unaddressed hazards.

\section{Critical Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The patient never intentionally errs or mispronounces phonemes.
    \item The user of the software is always a trained SLP.
    \item The user is utilizing a hardware/operating system that is identified as compatible.
    \item The user is operating in Ontario and hosting sessions within reasonable working hours (no later than 12 am or earlier than 6 am).
    \item The user has the basic knowledge of how to use web-based software.
    \item The user does not make diagnoses without additional verification outside of the software.
\end{itemize}

\section{Safety and Security Requirements}

The following security, safety, and reliability requirements were drawn as a result of the hazard analysis process:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-HA1}: The system shall prompt the user when noisy (less than 15 dB) microphone inputs are passed in, and allow them to retry that input.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} The system should not take in corrupted data as it will degrade the quality of the results and misrepresent a patient’s standing. A safer alternative is to prompt the user of the error, reject the data, and offer a retry option when the issue is resolved. 15 dB is the standard signal to noise ratio aimed for a clear microphone signal \textcolor{red}{[ACCEPTABLE MIC NOISE]}.

\item \textbf{FR-HA2}: The system must back up all patient progress data and analytics at the end of each session. It must alert the user if the backup fails.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} In the event of a data breach, patient data is not lost.

\item \textbf{FR-HA3}: The speech engine must provide a confidence percentage for each phoneme deduction it reaches. (have a FR to connect this to)\\
\textbf{Rationale:} Upon some speech analysis, the results must be kept transparent so the SLP is confident to accept them. This way they can leverage their professional judgement to reject incorrect deductions and consider high confidence ones.

\item \textbf{FR-HA4}: The system must alert the user when WiFi connection becomes unstable. It must provide an offline version which records patient audio for when the WiFi recovers.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} If the network connection becomes unstable, the connection to any processing server may drop and the analysis system may become inactive. The SLP needs to know of this so they could take alternative measures instead of randomly finding out their session was not tracked. A metric for unstable will depend on the user’s hardware specifications and the server specifications.

\item \textbf{FR-HA5}: The system must be able to transfer from online to offline mode at all times. The transfer cannot be blocked by network or interface malfunctions.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} If the network connection drops, the system cannot freeze and must fail gracefully. This way, the session may continue with limited functionality while still allowing the SLP to save speech files, and access the interface.

\item \textbf{FR-HA6}: The system must have two factor authentication and a password recovery system in place.

\item \textbf{FR-HA7}: The system must preface all deductions and analytics with an acknowledgement that they may be incorrect and may only be accepted under the discretion of an SLP.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} The tool is purely assistive and software may not be 100\% correct. Therefore this disclosure is necessary to ensure users do not try to replace the judgement of SLPs with this.

\item \textbf{FR-HA8}: Prior to authorizing any audio recordings, a one-time confirmation code sent to the patient’s trusted device at the start of the session must be entered into the SLP interface.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} This serves as a method of consent for the clinic to record the patient during this session. It indirectly verifies that the SLP has their interface open to the correct patient as well.

\item \textbf{FR-HA9}: The system shall maintain an audit trail detailing the user, action, and time of all data accesses and manipulations.\\
\textbf{Rationale:} It is important to keep a traceable log of all user and system actions in the event of errors, data breaches or other undesired events.
\end{itemize}

\section{Roadmap}

Requirements 1, 3, 7 will be integrated within the capstone timeline. The core of the product is its speech analysis ability, so filtering inputs for noisy data is crucial. It is important to be transparent about the validity of a claim, which is why each deduction must be supported by a confidence level. This way an SLP can make a more informed judgement rather than blindly trusting the software. Full disclosure is needed at all times to ensure it does not impede the professional judgement of an SLP. 

The remaining requirements specify necessary security and reliability mechanisms that must be in place before the product is released. Elements like authentication and redundancy are especially crucial for real life clinic scenarios, but do not enhance the value of the project as a capstone. Instead, time is better spent enhancing the SLP interface of the system, and focusing on the speech analysis.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What went well while writing this deliverable?
    We went into this deliverable with a general idea of the expected hazards.
    In many of our prior discussions (both as a group and with our supervisors), we discussed many of the potential failure modes.
    As a result, we spent more time expanding on the failure modes we have previously identified rather than ideating from scratch.
    Our supervisors also gave us many jumping off points to identify failures and provided us with related reading material.
    \item What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how
    did you resolve them?
    We struggled with differentiating on what is a failure mode and what is actually a cause of the failure mode.
    For instance, we identified the potential issue of unique vocal patterns or accents causing an incorrect phoneme to be identified.
    However, we realized that the unmet requirement was that an incorrect phoneme was identified (accuracy).
    This meant that it was ultimately a redundancy in the failure mode: Phonemic identification model misclassifies patient speech.
    To resolve this, we started rooting out the specific requirements that were not met in our ideas.
    We then separated the cause of failure out of this statement to reorganize our thoughts.
    \item Which of your listed risks had your team thought of before this
    deliverable, and which did you think of while doing this deliverable? For
    the latter ones (ones you thought of while doing the Hazard Analysis), how
    did they come about?
    We have had many discussions about risks associated with phonemic identification both in terms of accuracy and performance.
    We also had general discussions about sensitive patient data being lost or accessed by an actor who should not be able to access it.
    However, during the actual identification of the failure modes, we separated the data loss and incorrect access into more subtypes, such as a data breach or authentication failure.
    We then identified two additional risks, which were replacement of a clinician's judgement and patient frustration.
    We came up with these during analysis of our non-functional requirements, specifically medical requirements for the clinician's judgement and usability requirements for patient frustration.
    \item Other than the risk of physical harm (some projects may not have any
    appreciable risks of this form), list at least 2 other types of risk in
    software products. Why are they important to consider?
    One type of risk is the spread of misinformation. Many software products are used to provide people with media access.
    If the information shared by that media is incorrect or exaggerated, it can lead to people making drastic decisions that are not in their best interest or are unsafe.
    Another type of risk is a data breach. Software products are frequently entrusted with people's data.
    If that data is a vulnerability for them, it can be used to take advantage of the person, or exposes something that puts them into danger.
    For instance, if an app has information about banking information, the exposure of that information can lead to someone accessing their finances.

\end{enumerate}
