The following document outlines the development plan for the \textbf{Ghoti} capstone project. It contains all administrative project 
details including, confidentiality, IP, team member information and communication protocols. It also includes project technical plans and 
standards, including the Proof of Concept design, expected technologies and details on AI usage.

\section{Confidential Information}
Machine learning training uses publicly available speech corpora. Patient
recordings and related clinical data collected during use are personal
health information. Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act
(PHIPA) governs how health information custodians collect, use, disclose,
and safeguard that information.

Any royalties and revenues generated by the product will be equally distributed amongst Ghoti's licensees under the terms of each non-exclusive license.

\section{Team Meeting Plan}

\indent In-person team meetings will be held weekly on Thursdays from 3:30pm to 4:20pm. The purpose of these meetings will be to discuss upcoming deliverables, progress updates, and potential blockers. They will also be used to consolidate any questions that team members may have for the instructional team and project supervisors.

\indent There will also be biweekly meetings held with the project supervisors on Fridays from 9:00am to 10:00am. They are planned to be in-person but may be shifted to virtual Microsoft Teams meetings based on availability. These will be used to ensure the project is in line with the advisors’ requirements, and to address any questions that the development team might have.

\indent The meeting chair (Sarah Dorfman) will prepare an agenda for the main topics of discussion and distribute it to the team at least one day prior to any team meetings to give all members time to prepare. All members are encouraged to take notes during the meeting. After the meeting, the team will collectively assign action items to each other. If there is a supervisor meeting planned for that week, the team will also plan a corresponding agenda. 


\section{Team Communication Plan}

\indent The team will use Microsoft Teams as the main avenue of communication. All group members are required to keep notifications on so that emergencies can be addressed in a timely manner. It will be used to discuss ideas, share files, and join informal meetings when needed. 

\indent To supplement these informal discussions, the group will also maintain Github issues to track tasks in a more formal manner. Issues should clearly describe the task, come with an appropriate label, and be assigned to the relevant people. This is not necessary for issues concerning lectures, or team meetings, as it is assumed that all team members attend these.

\indent Communication with project supervisors will be done through McMaster Outlook emails. Based on the nature of the interaction, communication with the instructional staff may be done through McMaster Outlook emails, or through the discussion boards on Avenue to Learn.


\section{Team Member Roles}

To ensure the team works collaboratively and effectively, we have defined unique roles for each member. Each role has its own clearly 
defined tasks and responsibilities that reflect the team's goals and individual strengths of team members. While each team member will 
have a primary area of focus, these roles are not fixed and may evolve as the project progresses and team members rotate responsibilities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Project Manager \& CI/CD/Admin Lead:} This role oversees and manages the project timeline, team organization and progress. 
  The project manager maintains the CI/CD pipeline for building and
  deploying \LaTeX{} documentation. They also manage repository permissions,
  create issues, track progress, and organize milestones and deadlines.
  \item \textbf{Team Liaison:} This role is the main point-of-contact between the team and course instructors. They are responsible for 
  submitting/creating pull requests for deliverables, and facilitating communication of feedback and comments between the instructor and the 
  group. 
  \item \textbf{Requirements Expert:} This role leads communication with project stakeholders. They will lead the gathering of system and 
  stakeholder goals and lead the process of translating them into clear requirements to support the writing of the Software Requirements 
  Specification (SRS) documentation, ensuring traceability between stakeholder goals, system requirements and the final design. 
  \item \textbf{Systems Architect/Development Lead:} This role will ensure that the high-level system architecture is followed and oversees 
  the core implementation of the software. They will ensure the team adheres to the defined architecture and design principles and ensure 
  that the system is designed with extensibility to accommodate changes and project expansion.
  \item \textbf{Machine Learning (ML) Lead:} This role is responsible for overseeing the design, implementation, and evaluation of the 
  project's machine learning components. They will be the primary manager/liaison for datasets, model training, and performance evaluation. 
  \item \textbf{User Interface \& Experience (UI/UX) Lead:} This role is responsible overseeing the design and implementation of the user 
  interface. They will be the primary manager/liaison for the creation of wireframes, prototypes and the project's adherence to 
  human-centred design. 
\end{itemize}


\section{Workflow Plan}

The team uses short development cycles so stakeholders can review changes and
provide feedback as the product evolves.

Feature and fix branches are merged into \texttt{main} after review, and
releases are made from \texttt{main}. Each change starts with a GitHub issue
tracked in GitHub Projects. A developer takes the issue, creates an
issue-numbered branch, and references the issue in the pull request. For
example, the branch for
\href{https://github.com/lilyporter/Ghoti/issues/32}{issue \#32}
starts with \texttt{32-}. The pull request for that branch should reference
\texttt{\#32}.
At least one other team member reviews each pull request before merge.
After merge, the developer closes the issue and marks it Done in GitHub
Projects. The team prefers pull requests of no more than 500 changed lines
so they remain manageable to review.

GitHub Actions builds the \LaTeX{} documentation. The team plans to add
application formatting, unit test, and dependency checks, as well as model
accuracy, regression, and latency checks.

\section{Proof of Concept Demonstration Plan}

During the demo, children will speak live while completing guided practice
items on the iPad. A local server will analyze their speech and return the
recognized phonemes and detected errors for the clinician to review. This
walkthrough serves as a guide and can evolve as the prototype develops.

The team will assess whether the results reflect what was spoken and arrive
soon enough for the clinician to use during the session. If recognition is
unreliable or feedback arrives too late, the team will document where the
workflow breaks down and prioritize those fixes before expanding the demo.

\section{Expected Technology}

\subsection{Languages and Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Python}: speech processing, model evaluation and training, and application backend.
\item \textbf{JavaScript:} user interface.
\item \textbf{SQL:} data storage and access control.
\item The web application runs in Safari on iPad. A local server performs
  speech analysis.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Libraries and Frameworks}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Backend}: a Python web framework (e.g., FastAPI)
  with data validation (e.g., Pydantic).
\item \textbf{Frontend}: a JavaScript framework (e.g., React), an API to capture audio from the browser.
\item \textbf{Data}: a self-hosted database with row-level access control.
\item \textbf{Speech and machine learning}: PyTorch, the Hugging Face
  Transformers library, and standard audio-processing libraries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Audio Processing and Storage}

The iPad Safari interface sends recorded speech over an encrypted connection
to a local server for analysis. The server returns the results to the
frontend. Audio from every completed attempt is stored in a self-hosted
database, encrypted at rest, and linked to the attempt. Authorized SLPs can
replay saved audio across sessions for as long as the clinic's retention
policy permits.

\subsection{Pre-trained Models and Training}

\begin{itemize}
\item We expect to start from pre-trained speech models (e.g., wav2vec2) and existing phone recognizers, to train a model, if the existing solutions are found to not be good enough.
\item Training data will be publicly available child-speech corpora with phonetic annotations
\item Both existing commercial and open-source models are evaluated and are a fallback if a trained model does not meet the agreed targets
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Components we expect to implement ourselves.}
\begin{itemize}
\item comparison of recognized phones against the target sound
\item an evaluation harness for accuracy, speed, and regression testing
\item the application: practice interface, curriculum, and clinician dashboard.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Development Tooling}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Version control and planning}: Git for version control, GitHub
  for issue tracking and pull request review, and GitHub Projects for
  scheduling and progress tracking.
\item \textbf{Linting and formatting}: Ruff for Python and Prettier for
  JavaScript.
\item \textbf{Unit testing}: pytest for Python and Vitest for JavaScript.
  The team plans to review coverage reports to identify untested code.
\item \textbf{Continuous integration}: GitHub Actions builds the
  documentation. The application lint, format, and test checks listed in
  the Workflow Plan are planned. A pull request must receive reviewer
  approval and pass the required checks before it can be merged.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Measurement}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accuracy}: phone error rate against reference transcriptions;
  precision and recall for substitution, omission, and addition detection;
  agreement with speech-language pathologist annotations.
\item \textbf{Speed}: real-time factor (processing time divided by audio
  duration) and end-to-end latency, measure at each model's size to map the accuracy-versus-latency tradeoff.
\item \textbf{Regression testing}: a fixed held-out evaluation set is re-run after every training run or model change, and a change is accepted only if results stay within agreed thresholds.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Areas Requiring Additional Training}

\begin{itemize}
\item Phonetics and speech sound disorders, with our SLP advisor.
\item Fine-tuning and evaluating speech models.
\item Database access-control design.
\end{itemize}

\section{Use of AI and LLMs}

Our team will use LLMs to support brainstorming, application development, and documentation. Tools such as Claude and ChatGPT may be used to explore design approaches, explain concepts, and  write code. In the coding process, agents will also be implemented to improve workflow and follow best practices. Team members will make and take responsibility for all project decisions and submitted work.

We will review any generated code before including it in the application. We will check that it meets the project requirements, follows our design, and works as intended by running relevant builds, checks, and tests. Any output that cannot be verified or does not meet these checks will be revised or discarded.

\section{Coding Standard}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Python}: PEP 8, enforced with Ruff for linting and formatting
\item \textbf{JavaScript}: the Airbnb JavaScript Style Guide, enforced with Prettier.
\item \textbf{Commits and pull requests}: descriptive commit messages, one
  pull request per feature or fix, and at least one reviewer approval before
  merging to the main branch.
\item The team plans to enforce the application lint and format standards
  through required GitHub Actions checks on pull requests.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}

\input{../Reflection.text}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Why is it important to create a development plan prior to starting the
    project?}
    Much of the lost time in any project, especially a group project, is due to disorganization and poor coordination.
    Developing that organizational framework ensures that the team is on track to complete the project in a timely manner.
    The value of development planning rather than jumping straight into the code, is ensuring that more of the thinking is done sooner.
    The later work is instead coordinated implementation. Communication is one of the most challenging parts of engineering.
    We can ensure that we all know our expectations of the project, and we can resolve miscommunication early on.
    \item \textbf{In your opinion, what are the advantages and disadvantages of using
    CI/CD?}
    CI/CD automates much of the menial labour in software development.
    This lets the team focus on more critical aspects of the work instead of getting bogged down by the friction of testing and deployment.
    It is also a significant time saver.
    Many mistakes, which may be missed by human reviewers, will be caught by the CI/CD pipeline.
    This means reviewers can focus on architectural and design details as opposed to spending time on trivial things, such as naming conventions. \\
    On the other hand, the main disadvantage of implementing CI/CD is the initial time spent setting it up.
    This is especially true for us, since none of us have implemented CI/CD in our own projects, even though we may have worked in codebases that had CI/CD pipelines during our co-ops.
    Spending too much time on the perfect CI/CD pipeline is time lost on the features of the project. 
    \item \textbf{What disagreements did your group have in this deliverable, if any,
    and how did you resolve them?}
    We discussed where to process speech and store recordings. We chose
    a local server for processing and a self-hosted database for
    recordings. Audio is encrypted at rest, and authorized SLPs can replay
    recordings across sessions under the clinic's retention policy.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Appendix --- Team Charter}

\subsection*{External Goals}

The team's goals for this project revolve around how useful our product will be to stakeholders. By the end of the capstone, Ghoti is hoping to have created a tool that its supervisors and stakeholders will be using and experimenting with in their labs and clinics. Ghoti also hopes that if the product is trusted, it will be an asset for team members to acquire future employment and bode well for final grades in the capstone course. 

\subsection*{Attendance}

\subsubsection*{Expectations}

Team members are expected to attend all meetings at assigned times. Meetings are booked solidly in advance every Thursday from 3:30pm to 4:30pm. If no cancellations or prior exceptions have been given, all members are expected to attend. 

\subsubsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

Acceptable excuses for missing meetings: 

\begin{itemize}
  \item Family/personal issues or events 
  \item Personal events (given 1-3 days' notice depending on the current capstone situation) 
  \item Personal happenings with school (e.g., midterms, group assignment meetings, etc.) (Must be given 1-3 days' notice depending on the current capstone situation)
\end{itemize}

Should a team member have anything that is required for them to contribute to in a meeting, they should contribute as much as they're able to before they miss the meeting. 

Acceptable excuses for missing deadlines 
\begin{itemize}
  \item Family/personal issues or events 
  \item Personal happenings involving school (e.g., midterms, group assignment meetings, etc.) (Should notify the group ASAP if you realize you are going to miss a deadline due to any school factors). 
\end{itemize}

Missing one deadline is not the end of the world; missing numerous is a pattern and will be considered a problem that will need to be brought up with Dr. Smith. Team members should reach out as early as possible if they realize they are going to miss a deadline so the team can act quickly to help. 
\subsubsection*{In Case of Emergency}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Miss a meeting: Team members should contribute as much as they can before the meeting; if required, team members will designate work assigned in the meeting to the missing member and will notify them of this once the meeting is finished. 
  \item Can't meet a deliverable: Team members should contribute what they can but let the team know of the situation ASAP. The team will reassign work to complete the deliverable on time. Life happens, but poor communication or repeated failures may involve team discussion or discussions with Dr. Smith. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

\subsubsection*{Quality} 
\begin{itemize}
  \item Quality of Team Meetings: Team members are expected to show up with a general idea of what will be on the agenda for any given meeting. If there is any material or information team members need to have prepared before the meeting, they are expected to be prepared with said materials. Should a team member find themselves unable to properly prepare for a meeting, they should notify their team members as soon as possible and work with the team to find a solution. Situations like these can be forgiven as long as they are communicated ahead of time and not part of a greater pattern of failing to deliver. If a team member is assigned to take meeting notes, the notes should be concise and cover all key discussion points. 
  \item Quality of Deliverables: Each team member should complete their assigned portions of deliverables (barring accepted excuses), focusing as much as they can on producing quality work. Deliverables should be reviewed by each team member, and inconsistencies/issues should be brought up with the individual(s) responsible for creating them. Team meetings or discussions may be needed if the inconsistencies/issues are contested topics that cannot be resolved solely by the involved parties.  
\end{itemize}
\subsubsection*{Attitude}

Each team member should bring the attitude of wanting the team to succeed in the goals it set out to achieve. The actions that team members take should be meant to uplift the project and team as a whole and not tear each other down. Specifics are found in our code of conduct and conflict resolution plan: 
\begin{itemize}
  \item Code of Conduct: 
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Team members should treat each other with respect and dignity. No personal attacks or insults are allowed in team discussions. 
    \item Having fun/joking around is fine as long as it doesn't degrade the quality of a meeting or cause discomfort in team members. 
    \item Team members should communicate quickly and often; the quicker issues are communicated, the quicker they can be resolved. 
  \end{itemize}
  \item Conflict Resolution Plan: 
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Issues between team members should be discussed individually first; calling each other out in team meetings shouldn't be the go-to method of discussing issues unless it is warranted.
    \item Should no resolution be found between individuals, conflicts will be raised to the team level for discussion.
    \item Should team discussions fail, the issue will be brought to the TAs/Prof for resolution.
    \item Conflicts of a serious or harmful nature should be directly brought up to the professor for the safety of the parties involved.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}


\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

The team will use tentative deadlines to keep each other on track for team deliverables. During team meetings, we will discuss current progress, roadblocks, and upcoming deadlines to ensure everyone is on the same page with the work they have been assigned. A team member who is confused about the work expected from them should immediately reach out to the group for clarification. Team wins should be recognized throughout the capstone project's development, and shoutouts should be given to team members who are doing well in their work. If there are team members who are performing below expectations, the team should come together to address the issues hindering them. If necessary, a plan should be made for the team member that outlines steps for improvement. Should a team member fail to meet two deadlines for project deliverables, frequent or daily check-ins will be required to keep them on track and monitor their progress. Should failures to meet project deliverables continue, discussions with the Dr. Smith will be arranged for next steps. 
 
Attendance will be taken at each scheduled team meeting and will be measured for metrics. Below 80\% attendance to meetings, team members will be flagged after each meeting, below 60\% team discussions will occur and improvement plans might be created. Below 50\%, a meeting with the TA will be arranged. Below 30\%, a meeting with Dr. Smith will be arranged. 
 
The team will divide work equally as it relates to GitHub issues. If everyone is doing their assigned share of GitHub issues, it will be considered as the work being equally shared. If the team finds out that there is an unfair balance of work given to a team member, the team will reconvene to redistribute tasks.  
\
Should the team meet targets early we will all celebrate by going out for lunch. 

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

The team will go out for a trivia night or a similar team-building activity at least once per semester.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making} 

\begin{itemize}
  \item Individual decisions: Should decisions need to be made at a lower level, at least one to two other people should discuss available options and come to a consensus. If a consensus can't be reached, escalate to the whole team, where a majority vote will result in a decision being made.
  \item Team decisions: These should be immediately handled at the team level and will be sorted out with a majority vote resulting in a decision being made. 
  \item Any team member not present at a meeting where a decision is made should be notified of the decision and given the opportunity to provide feedback. If the team members involved in the decision feel that the feedback is warranted, they will reconvene to discuss the feedback and make a final decision.
\end{itemize}
