\subsection{Mathematical Notation} \label{sec:phoneme-notation}

Let \textit{x} represent the expected phoneme that the child is trying to produce.\\
Let \textit{y} represent the phoneme recognized from the child's recorded speech.\\
  \begin{equation}
    \mathrm{features}(\mathrm{phoneme}) = p,\quad p \in \{-1,0,1\}^n,\quad n>0
  \end{equation}
\noindent Note: \textit{p} is a representation of \textit{n} phonological features of the phoneme.\\ \\
\noindent Extract the phonological features of the expected and recognized phoneme:
\begin{equation}
  A = features(x)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  B = features(y)
\end{equation}
\noindent Compare using Manhattan distance to measure the difference between the feature vectors \cite{manhattan_distance_sd}:
\begin{equation}
  d_m(A,B) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} |A_i - B_i|
\end{equation}

For the heat map, phoneme closeness is $1 - d_m(A,B)/(2n)$, where
0 represents the least close pair and 1 the closest pair. Manhattan distance
supports FR-RDV5 because each feature contributes its absolute difference
with equal weight. The score reflects the size and number of feature
differences, and normalization gives the heat map a common scale. Recognition
confidence is reported separately. Omissions and additions have no phoneme
pair to score.

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:introduction}

A child’s phonemic inventory (the sounds they are able to make) typically stabilizes by ages 4--6 \cite{kid_sense_dev}, but this is not the case for many. In Ontario, roughly 1 in 10 children need extra help developing their speech and language skills \cite{ontario_slp_facts}. Receiving proper treatment has proven to improve their self-esteem, ability to socialize, and readiness for school \cite{ontario_slp_facts}. The job of an SLP is to provide this treatment: they assess a child’s speech, fluency, and articulation, and provide corresponding strategies to overcome their concerns.

The product PronouncedFish is an assistive tool intended for SLPs to streamline their workflow, and offer complementary insights to assist with their consultations. This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) highlights all functionalities and expectations that guide the application’s design. The current section describes the purpose of this document, project scope decisions, characteristics of the intended reader for the SRS, and a roadmap describing the topics covered by each section of the document.

\subsection{Purpose of Document}
The SRS will provide the formal requirements specification that any iteration of the product must adhere to. It compiles functional and non-functional requirements elicited from SLPs, children, and other relevant user groups that will guide the core development of this application. It will also outline some elements that are likely and unlikely to change through renditions of the product due to varying scope decisions, stakeholder needs, and user goals. A full list of goals and stakeholders can be found in the \href{https://github.com/lilyporter/Ghoti/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem Statement and Goals Document}.

\subsection{Scope of Requirements} 

Pediatric speech is very complicated because children are still learning how to coordinate their breath, lips, and brain to make certain sounds. This means it is normal for them to make mistakes around this age. By age 5, these features have generally stabilized so if a child is still making errors around this time, it suggests a need for speech therapy. Therefore, the product narrows down the target user space to children aged 4--6.

The product focuses on three speech error types: substitutions, omissions,
and additions. In a substitution, one phoneme replaces another, as when a
child pronounces \textit{rabbit} as \textit{wabbit}. An omission leaves out
an expected phoneme, and an addition introduces an extra one.

Another factor that complicates analysis of a child's speech is their accent. Accents are tuned to pronounce certain sounds in certain ways, causing significant deviations between youth from different geographical regions. Therefore, the user space has been further narrowed to monolingual speakers of North American English.

PronouncedFish supports two SLP-supervised modes for monolingual North
American English speakers aged 4--6. Testing uses Goldman--Fristoe test
items. Guided practice uses separate items and excludes the test words.
Children participate without accounts, and only authorized SLPs access
results and session history.


\subsection{Characteristics of Intended Reader} \label{sec_IntendedReader}

This document acts as the bridge between the actual application and the project developers. The reader is expected to have the equivalent software design understanding of a McMaster University Level 3 Software Engineering student. They must have a first year engineering level understanding of linear algebra and vector difference. This is important to understand the accuracy and comparisons of different speech sounds. A very basic understanding of linguistics terminology is also needed. They must know what a phoneme is, have an idea of some common speech sound errors (i.e. SODA), and understand the purpose of an SLP.

guided practice or testing and review results. The child responds to the

\subsubsection{Context of Typical Use}

The software will typically be used in medical applications for treating patients that have speech sound disorders (SSDs). The system is neither safety-critical nor mission-critical, should the system fail, users will not be in danger and critical business/societal infrastructure will not be impacted. Since PronouncedFish is primarily an assistant to SLPs, it is not autonomously making critical medical decisions. Should the system fail during treatment, the SLP can step in with their own judgements to correct issues that come from the system. Any administrative suggestions made by PronouncedFish can be overruled by an SLP's own judgement, helping to mitigate risk for end users.

\subsection{User Characteristics} \label{SecUserCharacteristics}

\subsubsection{Patient}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A patient is typically a child aged 4--6 with a speech sound disorder that may involve substitutions, omissions, or additions.
    \item Children respond to visual cues and stories told by the SLP. The activities require no reading.
    \item The child participates during the session. Detailed results and progress information are available through the SLP dashboard.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{SLP}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Expert in speech language pathology, they have a good understanding of phonemic analysis and are able to detect SSDs like substitutions.
    \item SLPs run sessions for patients, they decide which lessons to provide and which tools to use during a session to diagnose and treat SSDs. They have a good understanding of these lessons and tools and know how to employ them effectively.
    \item SLPs are able to track and analyze a patient's data. They are able to use data to inform them of the SSDs a patient may have and how best to treat it. They can also use the data to track the progress of treatment for a patient and use that information to guide them on which tools are best to use for future sessions.
    \item SLPs are likely not experts in machine learning, they should not need an understanding of how machine learning works to use PronouncedFish. 
    \item SLPs use their judgement to create lesson plans tailored to each patient's needs.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}

\textbf{Rationale:} This supports the devices and browser used for supervised sessions.
\item[\stepcounter{scnum}\scthescnum \label{Local Server Connectivity}: ] The iPad shall connect to the local server for speech processing and access to session data.\\

\paragraph{Recording limitation.}
Background noise can obscure the child's speech, clipping can distort it,
and an incomplete or overlong capture can exceed the supported duration
limits. FR-AC3 requires the system to flag these recordings and offer
the SLP a retry. The recording-quality validation defines the acceptable
background noise for recognizing the child's speech on the supported iPad
setup.

\section{Specific System Description} \label{sec:specific-system}

\subsection{Problem Description} \label{Sec_pd}

PronouncedFish is intended to solve the problem of identifying speech sound errors in young children during speech therapy sessions. 
Around 440,000 Canadians live with a speech, language, or communication disorder \cite{cdac2026statistics}, 
including 7-10\% of preschool children \cite{gaines2006earlyid}. 
There are several types of speech sound errors that sessions with an SLP can help with. Identifying errors, and tracking whether 
they improve over time, is a core part of an SLP's work with a patient.

Current speech analysis tools that support SLPs are limited. According to the project supervisors, some tools do not perform 
well with both speed and accuracy, especially on children's voices. Additionally, current tools and models are not tailored to 
provide real-time and interpretable analysis to an SLP during a speech therapy session. 

\subsubsection{Physical System Description} \label{sec_phySystDescrip}

PronouncedFish is a software system, but it is used within a physical setting. The physical setting for the software is a pediatric 
speech therapy session run by an SLP. 
The elements of the system's setting are described below.

\begin{itemize}

\item[PS1:] The SLP
\item Runs and supervises the therapy session.
\item[PS2:] The patient (children aged 4--6)
\item Attends the therapy session, receiving cues and direction from the SLP, using the aid provided by PronouncedFish.
\item[PS3:] Clinic technology
\item Safari on iPad captures speech and presents the clinician interface.
  The local server processes attempts, and the self-hosted database stores
  patient records and completed attempts.

\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Goal Statements}

\noindent A detailed list of \href{https://github.com/lilyporter/Ghoti/blob/main/pdfs/ProblemStatementAndGoals/ProblemStatement.pdf}{Problem Statement and Goals} may be found here. For PronouncedFish, the goal statements are:

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{GS1}: Identify substitutions, omissions, and additions in a child's production of a practice or test item.
\item \textbf{GS2}: Present the SLP with the results, including how the produced sounds differ from the expected sounds.
\item \textbf{GS3}: Track a patient's performance on target sounds across sessions.
\item \textbf{GS4}: Support the SLP in leading guided practice and testing with separate item sets.

\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Scope Decisions}

The following decisions are made to narrow down the scope of the project:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SD1}: The target demographic will be children aged 4--6.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} A child’s phonemic inventory (the sounds they are able to speak) typically stabilizes by ages 4--6 \cite{kid_sense_dev}. If a child has speech development issues at this point, they are referred to an SLP, making this a logical age range to choose. There is a lot more variation in speech at younger ages which may be normal, so younger age ranges are unrealistic to analyze for a capstone.

    \item \textbf{SD2}: The children will be monolingual speakers of North American English.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Speech recognition tools struggle with recognizing mistakes in bilingual people or with accented speech \cite{bias_accented_speech}. These speakers incorrectly get flagged as having speech disorders more often due to a lack of training data available for them \cite{bias_accented_speech}. This means a general all-encompassing speech analysis model is unrealistic, and the project must focus on a space with sufficient training data to work from.

    \item \textbf{SD3}: Detect substitutions, omissions, and additions.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} These error types can be described by comparing expected and recognized phoneme sequences. Distortion detection is a stretch goal.

    \item \textbf{SD4}: Support Safari on iPad for the interface.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Supervisors reported using iPads in sessions. Speech analysis runs on a local server.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Assumptions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A1: Item-based attempts.} Each attempt corresponds to a single selected practice or test item.
\item \textbf{A2: Speaker population.} Each speaker is a monolingual North American English-speaking child aged 4--6.
\item \textbf{A3: Expected phoneme sequence.} Each item has a predefined expected phoneme sequence $T = (t_1, \ldots, t_n)$.
\item \textbf{A4: Acceptable pronunciation variants.} Each item has a finite set of acceptable phoneme sequences that includes $T$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item These sets are established in consultation with an SLP advisor to account for acceptable pronunciation variation.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{A5: Substitution errors.} A substitution pairs an expected phoneme with a different recognized phoneme.
\item \textbf{A6: Omissions and additions.} An expected phoneme with no recognized counterpart is omitted. A recognized phoneme with no expected counterpart is added.
\item \textbf{A7: SLP supervision.} Each session is conducted under the direct supervision of an SLP.
\item \textbf{A8: Unintentional pronunciation errors.} All pronunciation errors made by the speaker are assumed to be unintentional.
\item \textbf{A9: Recording conditions.} Each recording contains speech from only one speaker and is captured in a quiet environment with minimal background noise.
\item \textbf{A11: Session devices.} Sessions use Safari on an iPad with a microphone and access to the local server.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Theoretical Models}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{TM1: Phoneme recognition.} $R(x) = (P, \mathbf{c})$, where $x$ is the recorded audio, $P = (p_1, \ldots, p_m)$ is the recognized phoneme sequence, and $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, \ldots, c_m)$ are per-phoneme confidence scores with $c_j \in [0, 1]$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Abstract mapping from audio to phonemes with confidence.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{TM2: Phoneme sequence comparison.} Align the recognized sequence $P$ with the expected sequence $T$ or an acceptable variant. The alignment supports paired phonemes, unmatched expected phonemes, and unmatched recognized phonemes.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{General Definitions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{GD1: Speech errors.} A mismatched aligned pair is a substitution. An unmatched expected phoneme is an omission. An unmatched recognized phoneme is an addition. A recognized sequence that matches an acceptable pronunciation does not produce a speech error.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Data Definitions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{DD1: Word position.} $\mathrm{pos}(i)$ is \textit{beginning} if $i = 1$; \textit{end} if $i = n$; otherwise \textit{middle}.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Report substitutions and omissions at their expected positions. Locate additions before, between, or after expected phonemes in the word.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Requirements} \label{sec:requirements}

This section provides the functional requirements, the business tasks that the

\subsection{Functional Requirements} \label{sec:functional-requirements}

\subsubsection{Audio Capture Requirements}

\noindent\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-AC1:} The system shall capture the patient's spoken response
for each attempt after an authenticated SLP confirms that valid consent
covers recording, retention, and later playback for the selected patient
and session (FR-HA8). \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Analysis requires a recording from the authorized session.
\item \textbf{FR-AC2:} The system shall use recorded audio to analyze its original attempt. It shall store the recording of every completed attempt encrypted at rest in a self-hosted database and link it to that attempt. The system shall allow authorized SLPs to replay it in the patient's history across sessions for the duration of the clinic's retention policy. \\\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can review how the patient produced a sound during an earlier session.
\item \textbf{FR-AC3:} The system shall flag a recording that fails its noise, clipping, or duration usability check and offer the SLP a retry (FR-HA1). \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can address the recording issue and request a new attempt.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Session Management Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-SM1:} The system shall allow the SLP to set up a session by selecting a patient, guided practice or testing, and the applicable targets and items. \\\textbf{Rationale:} The mode determines which items can be used, and the targets and items define what the analysis compares against.
\item \textbf{FR-SM2:} In either session mode, the system shall allow the SLP to start or end an attempt, repeat or skip an item, move to the next item, and end the session. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP controls the pace and can move on when an item is unsuitable.
\item \textbf{FR-SM3:} For each attempt, the system shall allow the SLP to enter and save the support level with that attempt. The levels are independent production, a semantic cue, and repetition or modeling. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Recording the amount of support lets the SLP distinguish independent from cued productions across sessions.
\item \textbf{FR-SM4:} The system shall allow the SLP to override any attempt classification as correct or incorrect and to correct individual phoneme results. It shall save the SLP's changes with the attempt while preserving the original analysis. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP interprets the results and can review the original analysis alongside corrections.
\end{itemize}

When the local server is unreachable, the system shall notify the SLP
and block new recordings. An attempt whose save has not been confirmed is
marked as interrupted. After reconnecting, the system preserves attempts
already saved on the server and lets the SLP retry an interrupted attempt
if it was unsaved (FR-HA4 and FR-HA5).

\subsubsection{Speech Analysis Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-SA1:} The system shall identify substitutions, omissions, and additions in recorded attempts. It shall record the expected and produced phonemes for substitutions, the expected phoneme for omissions, and the extra produced phoneme for additions. It shall include the word position or insertion location for each detected error. \\\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can identify what changed and where it occurred.
\item \textbf{FR-SA2:} The system shall accept pronunciations defined as acceptable variants for the selected item. \\\textbf{Rationale:} Acceptable variation should not be flagged as a speech error.
\item \textbf{FR-SA3:} The system shall report phoneme confidence and an attempt-level confidence score. It shall classify an attempt as ``unclassifiable'' when its attempt-level score is below the threshold established in the validation study. The study shall document how the attempt-level score is derived from phoneme confidence. \\\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can weigh uncertain results, and the system avoids forcing a classification on poor input, which reduces over-reliance on the tool.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Results \& Data Visualization Requirements}
\begin {itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-RDV1:} For each guided practice or testing attempt, the system shall show the SLP the expected phonemes and any recognized phonemes side by side. It shall identify unclassifiable attempts and distinguish an SLP override from the original analysis. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The system must present this data side by side so the SLP can compare and draw conclusions from them. 
\item \textbf{FR-RDV2:} The system shall show the SLP each identified substitution, omission, and addition in the expected-versus-recognized IPA comparison. A missing or extra sound shall be shown with a gap on the corresponding side. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The comparison helps the SLP see how the production differed from the expected item.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV3:} The system shall indicate the beginning, middle, or end of the word for substitutions and omissions and the insertion location for additions. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The location helps the SLP interpret the error.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV4:} In both session modes, the system shall show the SLP
counts of correct, incorrect, and unresolved unclassifiable attempts, using
the SLP classification override where present. It shall distinguish correct
independent attempts from correct cued attempts. A session with no attempts
shall show zero counts and no rates. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can review performance and how much support the child needed.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV5:} The system shall show phoneme closeness for eligible expected and produced pairs using the measure in Section~\ref{sec:phoneme-notation}. It shall show an empty heat map when no pairs can be scored. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Heat map shows produced phoneme closeness to expected phoneme for easy interpretation.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV6:} The current-session visualizations shall update when an attempt is completed, an SLP override changes, or the session ends. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can review the current results during the session and after it ends.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV7:} The system shall identify and present all visualizations as assistive and not diagnostic. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} All deductions are assistive, not diagnostic. It is important to remind users so they do not try using this to replace judgments of SLPs.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV8:} The system shall display error information only to
authorized SLPs through the clinician dashboard. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP decides how to discuss results with the child.
\item \textbf{FR-RDV9:} The system shall present phonemes as the appropriate IPA symbols. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} IPA provides a standard notation for representing speech sounds.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Progress \& Data Management Requirements}
\begin {itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-DMR1:} The system shall maintain, for each patient, a record of identifying information and session history. 
\item \textbf{FR-DMR4:} For every instance in which a patient's record is viewed, created, modified, exported or deleted, the system shall record the type of information involved, the date and time, the identity of the user, and the identity of the patient the information pertains to. The system shall retain prior audit events and the deletion event after the patient record is deleted. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Authorized audit users can review access and changes after the clinical record is deleted.
\item \textbf{FR-DMR5:} The system shall allow authorized users to retrieve the audit log and to provide a copy of it on request. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Authorized users can retrieve and copy the audit log for reference.
\item \textbf{FR-DMR6:} The system shall record, for each session, the date, the SLP, the target phonemes and words, the session results, and inputs from the SLP. 
\item \textbf{FR-DMR7:} The system shall associate every session and its results with exactly one patient, the patient selected for that session.
\item \textbf{FR-DMR8:} The system shall track each patient's performance on target phonemes over time. 
\textbf{Rationale:} Tracks each patient's target phoneme performance over time to make informed decisions about their progress and the effectiveness of therapy.
\item \textbf{FR-DMR9:} The system shall allow the SLP to compare a patient's correct production rate for a target phoneme between sessions. It shall show insufficient history if either session has no eligible observations for that target. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Allows the SLP to evaluate patient progress given their treatment.
\item \textbf{FR-DMR10:} The system shall report improvement and persistent errors for each target phoneme using the two latest sessions with eligible observations. The correct production rate is the number of accepted target productions divided by the number of eligible target observations. Substitutions and omissions count as incorrect. Additions and unresolved unclassifiable observations are excluded. Use phoneme-level SLP corrections when available. A higher rate indicates improvement, and errors in both sessions indicate persistence. Fewer than two eligible sessions shall yield no trend. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Identifies improving target phonemes and persistent error patterns.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Practice Activities Requirements}
\begin {itemize}
\item \textbf{FR-PAR1:} The system shall provide a set of practice items (words or phrases), each associated with the target phoneme(s) each one exercises and the position of that phoneme in the word. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Allows patients to participate in speech activities directly within the app.
\item \textbf{FR-PAR2:} The system shall provide at least three practice items for each target phoneme in each word position. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} Ensures there is sufficient practice material for patients to use. 
\item \textbf{FR-PAR3:} The system shall allow the SLP to select practice items by target phoneme and word position. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can choose practice items for the child's target phonemes.
\item \textbf{FR-PAR4:} Guided practice items shall be appropriate for the supported age range (4--6) and exclude Goldman--Fristoe test words. Testing mode shall use Goldman--Fristoe test items. The system shall allow SLPs to create guided practice lessons from the practice items.
\item \textbf{FR-PAR5:} For each guided practice item, the system shall provide a visual cue that the SLP can use to elicit the target response. The SLP can tell a story as part of the prompt.
\begin {itemize}
  \item \textbf{FR-PAR5.1:} Activities in guided practice and testing shall require no reading by the child.
  \item \textbf{FR-PAR5.2:} Visual cues shall be recognizable and appropriate for children ages 4--6.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Rationale:} Visual cues and SLP-told stories allow children to respond before the SLP models the target as a cue.
\item \textbf{FR-PAR7:} During guided practice, the system shall keep the visual cue for the current item available until the SLP changes the item or ends the session. \\
\textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can return the child's attention to the prompt during the activity.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Nonfunctional Requirements} \label{sec:nonfunctional-requirements}
    \subsubsection{Accuracy Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-ACU1:} The system shall achieve 87\% phoneme identification accuracy against the analysis of one or more speech-language pathologists.\\
        \textbf{Rationale: } Many models created after 2020 for phonemic analysis are able to achieve accuracies around the 80-90\% range \cite{auto_english_pron}.    
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Performance Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-PER1:} For a short sentence, the system shall display the phoneme results less than 10 seconds after the recording ends. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} The SLP can review the result during the session without a prolonged wait \cite{software_response_times}.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Usability Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-USA1:} Within one hour of first use, an SLP shall be able to sign in, select a patient, run a guided practice and a testing attempt, and find the saved results and playback in the patient's history without assistance. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} These tasks cover the clinician's session and follow-up workflow.
        \item \textbf{NFR-USA2:} The system shall require the SLP to confirm deletion of a patient record before deleting it.\\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Confirmation helps prevent accidental deletion of patient records.
        \item \textbf{NFR-USA3:} For the same SLP and a matched set of clinical tasks, the total time to record attempt outcomes and review session history with PronouncedFish shall be less than the time to complete those tasks with the SLP's usual manual workflow. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Comparing the same tasks for the same SLP controls for differences in workload and familiarity.
        \item \textbf{NFR-USA4:} Children shall participate only in SLP-supervised activities without a patient account or login. Results, progress, and recording playback shall be accessible only to authorized SLPs. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} SLP supervision and restricted access protect patient information.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Maintainability Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-MAI1:} The clinician interface shall be separable from the speech analysis component so that a change to the interface can be made without modifying speech analysis. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} This separation supports later interfaces for other professions.
        \item \textbf{NFR-MAI2:} The speech error classification shall be separable from audio capture and patient-record storage so that adding an error type does not require changes to those components. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} This separation supports later extension to other speech differences.
        \item \textbf{NFR-MAI3:} Building and deploying a patch to the local server shall take less than 30 minutes, measured from the start of the build to the first successful availability check after deployment. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} A short deployment window lets the team apply critical fixes between sessions.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Portability Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-POR1:} The interface shall support iPadOS. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} The iPad is the target session device.
        \item \textbf{NFR-POR2:} The interface shall support Safari on iPad. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Safari is the target browser for the supervised workflow.
        \item \textbf{NFR-POR3:} Guided practice and testing shall be operable in Safari on an iPad with a microphone and a connection to the local server. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Both modes require capturing and analyzing speech during a session.

    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Reliability Requirements}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-REL1:} For sign-in, sign-out, and patient-record saves and retrieval, the system shall return a response within 10 seconds of receiving the request. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} A bounded response time limits interruptions to the clinician's workflow.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Security and Privacy}
    Access to the clinician dashboard, patient records, or saved recordings
    requires SLP sign-in with two-factor authentication. SLP password recovery
    is available (FR-HA6 in the Hazard Analysis).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-SAP1:} The system shall encrypt stored personal health information, including recordings, at rest. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Encryption protects stored information from unauthorized access.
        \item \textbf{NFR-SAP2:} The system shall encrypt communications between system components. \\
        \textbf{Rationale:} Encryption protects information in transit between the iPad, the local server, and the database.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsubsection{Legal and Licensing:} 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{NFR-LAL1:} The system will follow the Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA) guidelines for data storage, data transfer, data access and data residency.\\
        \textbf{Rationale: } SLPs are required to work within the guidelines of PHIPA to protect patient information. Thus, the tools they use in sessions must also not violate PHIPA guidelines.
        \item \textbf{NFR-LAL2:} The system will be open source using the GNU General Public License (GPL).\\
        \textbf{Rationale:} The materials that are used to make PronouncedFish are licensed under the GNU GPL. Thus, PronouncedFish itself becomes licensed under GNU GPL and becomes open source.
    \end{itemize}


\section{Likely Changes} \label{sec:likely-changes}

Below is a list of requirements that are likely to change. The provided rationale will explain whether this is due to a scope decision, stretch goal, or other factor. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{LC1}: The system may be extended to identify distortion errors.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The capstone covers substitutions, omissions, and additions. Distortion detection requires additional work.
    \textbf{Related Assumption(s):} A5, A6

    \item \textbf{LC2}: The speech analysis model will fairly accommodate accents originating from regions outside North America. Fair implies the model will recognize errors at a comparable accuracy (+/- 5\% relative to the North American accent) for speakers of any accent.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Due to scope requirements, the supported population consists of monolingual speakers of North American English. English speakers are very diverse, and there must be adequate support for users from all backgrounds.\\
    \textbf{Related Assumption(s):} A2

    \item \textbf{LC3}: The system shall provide an interface for an SLP to assign homework to their patients.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The first iteration of the system will provide an interface for an SLP-led session in the clinic. A stretch goal is to allow patients to complete homework at home, but this is not a core feature, so it is a likely change.\\
    \textbf{Related Assumption(s):} A7

    \item \textbf{LC4}: The system shall highlight errors for children of all age ranges based on the standardized speech sound development chart.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Due to scope requirements, the target child age has been restricted to ages 4--6. Speech errors may occur at any age, and even for adults. Therefore, more flexibility will be crucial in future iterations of the product.\\
    \textbf{Related Assumption(s):} A2

    \item \textbf{LC5}: The web application may support more browsers and devices, and a later release may include native mobile apps.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Broader compatibility could support more clinic setups. The interface targets Safari on iPad.
    \textbf{Related Assumption(s):} A11
\end{itemize}

\section{Unlikely Changes} \label{sec:unlikely-changes}

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{UC1:} The system will shift its focus to working with adults instead of children.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} The goal of PronouncedFish is to help children with SSDs in the early stages of their phoneme-pronouncing journey. Switching to working with adults will require different models for phonemic identification and different datasets to train them to be effective.
    \item \textbf{UC2:} The system will change its guidelines from PHIPA to something else.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Since Ontarian SLPs mostly work with PHIPA, using a different guideline would be counterproductive and unrepresentative of the target SLP user base. The guidelines should only change if new laws are enacted that supersede PHIPA.
    \item \textbf{UC3:} The system will shift its focus away from English speakers.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} Whilst in theory possible for PronouncedFish to work with languages other than English, members of the team and the supervisors primarily work with English speaking patients, so the team will evaluate the selected recognition model on English child speech.
    \item \textbf{UC4:} The system will use generative AI to create lesson plans.\\
    \textbf{Rationale:} SLPs are the experts when it comes to knowing what a patient needs to treat their SSDs. While this feature is possible to implement, Ghoti entrusts the responsibility of creating new lesson plans with the SLPs due to their expertise. 

\end{itemize}

\section{Development Plan} \label{sec:development-plan}
\textbf{Proof of Concept:}
The Proof of Concept demonstrates live speech analysis during a guided
practice session on iPad. An SLP reviews the recognized phonemes and
detected errors as the local server processes each attempt.

\textbf{Phase 1:}
The Capstone Expo release supports SLP-supervised guided practice and
Goldman--Fristoe testing. It includes SLP-created practice lessons,
clinician-only results and progress views, and playback of every completed
attempt. Practice excludes Goldman--Fristoe test words. The iPad interface
runs in Safari, speech analysis runs on a local server, and a self-hosted
database holds encrypted recordings. SLP sign-in, two-factor authentication,
password recovery, SLP confirmation of recording consent, and
connection-loss handling with an SLP-initiated retry are in scope for Expo.

\textbf{Phase 2:}
Possible extensions include distortion detection and additional guided
practice activities.
\textbf{Phase 3:}
This phase prepares the system for clinical deployment, including review
of security, privacy, and PHIPA safeguards.
\textbf{Phase 4 and on:}
Future releases may extend the software to other professions and applications.
Additional phonemic analysis may be required for this to detect speech differences that do not fall under SODA such as prosody.
