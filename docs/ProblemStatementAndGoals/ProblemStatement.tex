\subsection{Problem}

Around 440,000 Canadians deal with some kind of speech, language, or communication issue []; this includes 7-10\% of preschool children in Canada [].
A common speech impediment in children is substitution, which is when an expected phoneme is replaced by another (e.g. a child may pronounce “Rabbit” as “Wabbit”) [].
For detection of said issues, speech language pathologists (SLPs) and researchers have used various tools for analyzing speech and helping individuals improve.
Current phonetic detection tools for researchers and SLPs are proprietary, not compliant with the Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA), and/or poor in performance (e.g. speed and accuracy) [CITE CONVERSATION WITH TURKSTRA DR PAPE].
This means that available tools are insufficient for research and for treating substitution speech impediments in children.
There should be a substitution treatment tool for child SLPs that is open-source, PHIPA-compliant, performant, and expandable to other professions (e.g. accent coaches) and other speech differences beyond execution.
