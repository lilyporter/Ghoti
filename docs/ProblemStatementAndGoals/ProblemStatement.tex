\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Problem}

Around 440,000 Canadians deal with some kind of speech, language, or communication issue \cite{cdac2026statistics}; this includes 7-10\% of preschool children in Canada \cite{gaines2006earlyid}.
Common speech sound errors in children include substitutions, omissions, and
additions. A substitution replaces an expected phoneme, as when a child
pronounces \textit{rabbit} as \textit{wabbit}. An omission leaves out an
expected phoneme, and an addition introduces an extra one \cite{asha2025ssd}.
Speech-language pathologists (SLPs) and researchers use tools to analyze speech and
help individuals improve. Trained professionals in the field identified barriers
in available tools, including proprietary access, privacy concerns, and
limitations in speed and accuracy \cite{supervisor2026initialcomm}.

These limitations create a need for an open-source tool that helps pediatric
SLPs identify speech sound errors quickly enough to guide supervised practice
and testing. It must protect patient information under Ontario's Personal
Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA). PronouncedFish supports clinicians
in reviewing speech patterns and tracking progress across sessions.

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}

\subsubsection{Inputs}

\textbf{Patient}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient's speech.} During a session with an SLP, the patient
      speaks into a microphone in response to guided practice or test items.
      These spoken responses are the input for phonetic analysis.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{SLP}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient selection.} The SLP selects the patient whose
      speech will be analyzed and whose progress will be tracked.
    \item \textbf{Session setup and lesson creation.} The SLP selects guided
      practice or testing, chooses target phonemes and items for the session,
      and creates lessons for guided practice.
    \item \textbf{SLP observations.} The SLP records cue levels and can correct
      the system's interpretation of an attempt.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Outputs}

\textbf{SLP}

The clinician dashboard provides the following outputs to the SLP.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phonemic speech analysis.} After an attempt is analyzed, the
      dashboard compares the phonemes the system recognized with the phonemes
      expected for the item. It presents both in IPA and flags substitutions,
      omissions, and additions.
    \item \textbf{Patient data and progress tracking.} The dashboard displays
      session summaries and detailed results so the SLP can see where the
      patient is progressing and which target phonemes still need practice.
    \item \textbf{Recording playback.} Authorized SLPs can replay audio from
      completed attempts in the patient's session history to review results
      across sessions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stakeholders}

\subsubsection{Primary Stakeholders}
\textbf{Speech-language pathologists.} SLPs are the main user group for this
application. PronouncedFish serves as an assistant during their everyday
activities. They can use it to assess speech patterns, identify which phonemes
a child has difficulty producing, and use the feedback to adjust treatment.
\vspace{1.0em}

\noindent\textbf{Patients.} Children interact with the application during
guided practice and testing with their SLP. Children may have limited
experience with digital tools, so activities should be age-appropriate and
easy to follow with the clinician's guidance.

\subsubsection{Secondary Stakeholders}
\textbf{Parents and guardians.} Parents and guardians have a direct interest
in how the application supports their child's care. They depend on accurate
feedback to the SLP and on safeguards for their child's recordings and other
personal information.

\subsubsection{Tertiary Stakeholders}
\textbf{Academia (linguistics students and cognitive science researchers).}
Future applications of the underlying speech-analysis tool could help
researchers study patterns in pediatric speech and give aspiring SLPs
examples to refer to while learning speech-language pathology.
\vspace{1.0em}

\noindent\textbf{Governments.} In Ontario, PHIPA protects patient confidentiality.
Governments have an interest in how the application handles and safeguards
patient data.

\subsection{Environment}

PronouncedFish is designed for supervised pediatric speech-language therapy
sessions. The iPad interface uses Safari, and a local server analyzes speech.

\noindent\textbf{Hardware}
\begin{itemize}
    \item iPad with a built-in or external microphone.
    \item Local computer to run the speech-analysis service.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Software}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Safari on iPad.
    \item An encrypted connection to the local service.
\end{itemize}

\section{Goals}

The overall objective of this project is to provide assistance to pediatric
SLPs during their patient sessions. PronouncedFish acts as an in-session
assistant, helping SLPs identify speech challenges quickly and tailor therapy
more effectively. The system is strictly assistive and supports the
clinician's professional judgment.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Enable an SLP to create a lesson and lead a patient through guided
      activities that target specific speech challenges. The SLP can also
      run a supervised testing session.
    \item Enable the SLP to record a patient's speech and review a
      comparison of the expected and recognized sounds during the session.
      The comparison identifies substitutions, omissions, and additions.
      Compare the flagged errors with SLP-reviewed reference examples and
      check that results arrive in time for the SLP to use them during the
      session.
    \item Enable the SLP to review visualizations of the sounds produced and
      the flagged speech patterns, and correct the system's interpretation
      when it differs from their clinical judgment.
    \item Enable authorized SLPs to review session records, replay completed
      attempts, and compare a patient's progress on target phonemes across
      sessions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stretch Goals}
The following are goals for possible expansion of the project after the main goals are achieved:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient-Facing/Homework Interface:} An additional interface for patients, allowing them to complete at-home practice activities to improve their speech.
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Account for different types of users (SLP \& patients).
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mobile Application:} An Android/iOS application for patients to practice their speech and complete homework activities on a mobile device.
    \item \textbf{Broader Speech Pattern Detection:} Identify distortion
      patterns and other speech differences beyond the current scope.
    \item \textbf{Automatic Lesson Recommendations:} Suggest practice
      lessons based on the patient's needs and session history.
    \item \textbf{Additional Interfaces:} Develop interfaces for other
      professions and uses of the underlying speech analysis model, such as
      an accent coach application.
    \item \textbf{OS Application:} Adapting the web-based system into an application that can be run natively on different operating systems (ex: Windows, Linux, macOS).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{What went well while writing this deliverable?} \\
    Before beginning the deliverable, the group discussed each of the required sections together and took detailed notes for each one. This helped with writing the deliverable because everyone in the group was on the same page and understood the approach we wanted to take. This allowed us to easily divide up the writing work and ensure that each section would be cohesive and unified. Additionally, the group set an internal deadline to complete the writing the day before the official deliverable due date. This helped keep us on track and accountable for our work.
    \item \textbf{What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how
    did you resolve them?}
    Since this was the first deliverable and the group's first time working with the LaTeX files in the repo template, there were a few initial challenges and technical difficulties with viewing the PDFs as they would be generated after editing the LaTeX files. We resolved this by communicating our challenges, helping each other, and trying different methods. 
    Additionally, after the initial meeting on the deliverable, there was a little further debate on what features should count as goals vs stretch goals and what the Proof of Concept (PoC) should encompass. This stalled the writing of those respective sections while the group engaged in further discussion to come to a consensus.
    \item \textbf{How did you and your team adjust the scope of your goals to ensure
    they are suitable for a Capstone project (not overly ambitious but also of
    appropriate complexity for a senior design project)?}
    After discussing our project idea with our supervisors, Daniel Pape and Lyn Turkstra (subject matter experts in SLP and speech analysis), we were able to narrow our scope for our PoC model. We came to understand that speech challenges encompasses several different categories and it would likely be difficult to target each one. We initially limited the PoC to substitutions. We later expanded the capstone scope to include omissions and additions.
    Additionally, we had ideas for multiple types of interfaces that could use our model (an SLP interface, a patient interface, an accent coach interface, etc). We ultimately decided to narrow our scope to focus on the SLP interface, the most important option given our overall project goal: assist speech language pathologists during speech therapy sessions. The other types of interfaces were relegated to stretch goals.

\end{enumerate}
